HomeThe doorway effect

Walking through a doorway can make your brain file a memory away.

You walked into the kitchen and forgot why. That is probably not a bad memory. Psychologists call it the doorway effect, and a well-known study suggests your brain may be doing it on purpose.

In a University of Notre Dame study, Professor Gabriel Radvansky found that people forgot what they were doing more often after walking through a doorway than after crossing the same distance inside one room. The result held in virtual rooms and in real ones, in the tasks his team used.

Why it might happen

The leading explanation: your brain stores memories in episodes. A doorway signals a scene change, so your brain closes the current episode and files it away, along with the reason you stood up. The memory is not deleted. It is filed in a room you are not in anymore.

The honest limit

Here is where we keep it real: a 2021 study in BMC Psychology ran four experiments and did not find the same general forgetting. So treat the doorway effect as shown in some tasks, not settled science. Your brain is doing something at doorways. Researchers are still arguing about exactly what.

The fix

Do not retrace your steps. Retrace your thoughts. Picture where you were, and what you were doing, when the idea hit. Rebuilding the scene in your mind may reopen the file, and in the original study walking back to the first room did not reliably help.
